Clinical Pastoral Education in Nigeria: A Contextual Framework for Professional Training, Quality Assurance, and Institutional Development

Rev. Chris Johnson, PhD (Christian Education)

Abstract

Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE) is an experiential approach to professional pastoral formation that integrates theological reflection, supervised encounters with people experiencing distress, interpersonal learning, and critical evaluation of pastoral practice. It prepares clergy, chaplains, and other spiritual caregivers to respond more competently to the spiritual, emotional, relational, and existential concerns that arise in healthcare and other institutional settings. In Nigeria, the development of CPE presents both an opportunity and a challenge: existing training initiatives provide a foundation for professional growth, while questions concerning institutional coordination, curriculum consistency, supervisory capacity, clinical placement, affordability, contextual relevance, and recognition require sustained attention. This paper proposes a contextually responsive framework for strengthening CPE in Nigeria. It adopts a conceptual policy-analysis approach informed by selected scholarly literature and publicly available institutional information. It does not present findings from a new national survey, interviews, or quantitative evaluation. Drawing on experiential learning, reflective practice, professional chaplaincy education, and spiritual-care literature, the paper argues that an effective Nigerian CPE framework should combine supervised clinical practice, structured theological and behavioural-science reflection, ethical competence, cultural and religious sensitivity, interdisciplinary collaboration, and transparent quality assurance. It proposes a phased implementation strategy involving stakeholder mapping, collaborative standards development, pilot programmes, educator development, outcome evaluation, and progressive institutionalisation. The paper concludes that the sustainable development of CPE in Nigeria requires collaboration among training providers, theological institutions, healthcare facilities, professional associations, and relevant public authorities. Such collaboration should preserve the distinctive contribution of pastoral care while ensuring that spiritual-care practitioners work ethically, competently, and collaboratively within multidisciplinary environments.

Keywords: Clinical Pastoral Education; Nigeria; chaplaincy; pastoral care; spiritual care; theological education; clinical supervision; quality assurance; professional formation.
